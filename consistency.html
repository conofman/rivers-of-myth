A Different Kind of Test

The Consistency Test

Belief is a porthole into character, not just religious belief, but the deeper habit underneath it: do you apply a principle because it's true, or because of who it currently benefits? Four constitutional scenarios below. Read the first version, form your honest reaction, then reveal the mirror, the same principle, same clause, opposite political direction.

This isn't a partisan scorecard and there's no score. It's a private check on whether your answer tracks the principle or tracks the team.

Nothing you do on this page is recorded, sent anywhere, or shared. It's just you and the four cards.
01
Due Process — Fifth & Fourteenth Amendments: no person shall be deprived of liberty without due process of law, a protection that applies to any person on U.S. soil, not only citizens.

Federal agents detain someone and begin removal proceedings based on their appearance and neighborhood, before any judge or hearing reviews the case.

Same clause, opposite direction

Federal agents detain a U.S. citizen and begin removal proceedings based on public statements they've made criticizing the government, before any judge or hearing reviews the case.

Same due-process clause, same "before a judge weighs in" structure. Did your answer change based on who's being detained, or on what due process actually requires?

02
First Amendment — freedom of speech, press, and peaceable assembly, protections that exist specifically to shield speech the government in power dislikes.

A federal agency revokes a news outlet's press credentials after it published a string of stories critical of the current administration.

Same clause, opposite direction

A federal agency revokes a news outlet's press credentials after it published a string of stories favorable to the political side you disagree with, under a past administration you opposed.

The First Amendment's entire purpose is protecting speech power doesn't like. If credential-pulling only bothers you when it happens to outlets you already agree with, that's not a free-press principle, it's a team preference.

03
Fourth Amendment — protection against unreasonable search and seizure without a warrant or individualized probable cause.

Law enforcement conducts warrantless searches of phones and homes concentrated in neighborhoods associated with a religious or ethnic minority, citing general security concerns.

Same clause, opposite direction

Law enforcement conducts warrantless searches of phones and homes concentrated in neighborhoods associated with gun owners or a political movement you personally belong to, citing general security concerns.

Same warrant requirement, same "general concern" justification. Notice whether your discomfort tracks the constitutional question or the specific group being searched.

04
Fourteenth & Fifteenth Amendments — equal protection under the law, and the right to vote regardless of race or other protected status.

A state controlled by the political party you support passes a law that makes voting meaningfully harder in districts that reliably vote against that party.

Same clause, opposite direction

A state controlled by the opposite party passes a law that makes voting meaningfully harder in districts that reliably vote against that party.

If the first law is "election security" and the second is voter suppression, the standard being applied isn't the Fourteenth or Fifteenth Amendment, it's who benefits.

The connection to the rest of this site isn't incidental. A tradition that trains people to distrust their own reasoning and defer to an unquestionable authority is training a muscle, and that muscle doesn't know the difference between a pulpit and a podium. Whether the answers above stayed consistent or flipped with the team is worth sitting with honestly, no one's watching, and no one's grading it.
