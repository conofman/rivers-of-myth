The Revelation Test

How can you know a message really came from God — and that it is true? There is no score and no trick. You tell us what you believe. We follow your answers.

This test does not try to prove Christianity false. It asks one simple question: if the Bible says false spiritual messages can happen, how can we know the messages in the Bible are the true ones?
Nothing you choose here is sent anywhere, saved, or graded. Your answers stay in this browser tab while you take the test.

Before we begin

When Christians have a strong answer, we will show it. We will not use weak replies just to make the test easier. You can also go back and change any answer.

We will keep three questions separate: Does God lie? Can God allow deception? and if false spiritual messages can happen, how do we tell the true ones from the false ones?

Sources & notes

This test uses the Bible itself and includes serious Christian replies, not just skeptical ones. If a verse or translation is debated, we say so and let you leave it out.

  • 1 Kings 22:19–23 — Micaiah's vision of the lying spirit; KJV is public domain.
  • 2 Thessalonians 2:11 — compare how different English Bibles translate the verse.
  • Ezekiel 14:9, NET notes — notes on the disputed wording. This test does not depend on the verse.
  • Deuteronomy 13:1–5 — even a sign that comes true is not enough by itself to prove a prophet is true.
  • 1 John 4:1 — “test the spirits.”
  • Galatians 1:8 — even an angel is rejected if the message goes against the gospel already received.
  • Robert B. Chisholm Jr., “Does God Deceive?”, Bibliotheca Sacra 155 (1998) — a conservative Christian scholar arguing that God can allow deception as punishment without making God a liar.
  • Gregory H. Harris, “Does God Deceive? The ‘Deluding Influence’ of Second Thessalonians 2:11”, Master's Seminary Journal 16 (2005).
  • Westminster Confession of Faith, ch. 1 §§4–5 — a classic Reformed explanation of why Scripture should be trusted and how the Holy Spirit confirms it.
  • John Piper, “Does God Lie?” — a conservative Christian answer: God does not lie, but may allow lying as judgment.
What this test does not claim: it does not prove that Moses, Isaiah, Paul, John, or any other Bible writer was deceived. It asks a simpler question: if false spiritual messages can happen, how can we know which messages really came from God?
